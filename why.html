Why chia pudding

Real ingredients,
real reasons.

Every jar is built from three functional ingredients — chia seeds, fresh fruit, and a creamy dairy-free base — chosen for what they actually do for your body, not just how they taste.

Ingredient 01

Chia seeds

A 1-ounce (about 2 tablespoons) serving of chia seeds provides roughly 10 grams of fiber, 5 grams of protein, and 5 grams of omega-3 alpha-linolenic acid — more omega-3 than an equal amount of whole flaxseed.

Fiber that works for you

Chia fiber can improve heart health, lower cholesterol, and support intestinal health. It slows digestion, which helps you feel full longer.

Heart-healthy fats

Chia's omega-3 fatty acids are linked to lower blood pressure, reduced platelet aggregation, and lower LDL cholesterol — supporting cardiovascular health.

Blood sugar support

Chia's soluble fiber can slow the absorption of sugar, which may help moderate blood glucose levels after eating.

Minerals in every jar

Chia seeds supply calcium, magnesium, manganese, phosphorus, and iron — a 1-ounce serving contains about 14% of the daily value for calcium.

Ingredient 02

Real fruit

Every jar uses whole fruit for flavor — mango, strawberry, apple, and blueberry — instead of syrups or artificial flavoring.

Naturally sweetened

Whole fruit provides natural sugars alongside fiber and water content, which helps moderate how quickly that sugar is absorbed compared to refined sweeteners.

Vitamins and antioxidants

Fruits like blueberries and strawberries are well known for vitamin C and antioxidant plant compounds, while mango adds vitamin A and additional vitamin C.

Ingredient 03

A creamy, dairy-free base

Oat milk and coconut milk combine to create a rich, creamy texture — naturally dairy-free.

Beta-glucan fiber

Oat milk contains naturally occurring beta-glucans, a soluble fiber shown to help manage cholesterol levels as part of a balanced diet.

Naturally dairy-free

If you're managing a dairy allergy or lactose intolerance, oat milk and coconut milk remove that concern entirely.

Medium-chain fatty acids

Coconut milk contains medium-chain fatty acids, including lauric acid, which supports immune function and may help improve cholesterol levels.

Used in moderation

We use just 2 teaspoons of coconut milk per jar — enough to add richness and nutrients without the very high fat and calorie content coconut milk has when used in large amounts.

Put together

Why the combination matters

No single ingredient makes a food "healthy" on its own. What matters is the combination: fiber from chia and oat milk working together, heart-supportive fats from chia and coconut milk, real fruit instead of syrup-based flavoring, and a naturally dairy-free base that doesn't require substitutions. Every jar is built to deliver fiber, healthy fats, and real nutrients — not empty calories.

These statements describe general, well-documented properties of chia seeds, oat milk, and coconut milk. They are not medical advice, and individual nutrition needs vary — see our Nutrition page for ingredient-specific values and a full disclaimer.
